The problem

Real solar observations do not have paired clean labels. A smoother image does not necessarily preserve faint signals better. This research examines that difference by auditing responses to known signal increments.

The approach

  1. Read FITS observations, check data quality and masks, and fix data splits and evaluation protocols.
  2. Adapt the authors’ SHINE architecture across three spatial/temporal input configurations and three seeds under a fixed training budget.
  3. Compare networks with classical baselines; retain configurations, checkpoints, hashes, and evaluation receipts to trace findings to artifacts.

Existing artifacts

  • Training completion records exist for 9 models, each trained for a fixed 6,000 steps.
  • Evaluation artifacts exist for 3 held-out dates, with output hashes and independent recomputation records.
  • An English manuscript and experiment figures have been drafted and remain in revision.

Findings & limitations

The narrow-probe comparison on the held-out dates did not show the temporal networks outperforming the Gaussian control. Known-increment responses are not clean-image accuracy and do not establish universal model superiority.

What comes next

  • Extend the existing minimal reproduction package with broader scientific evaluation.
  • Review shareable samples, third-party licenses, and individual contributions.
  • Extend the scientific input protocol and verify migration across machines.
